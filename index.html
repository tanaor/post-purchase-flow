Post-Purchase Upsell Flow

One-click offers shown after the card is charged and before the receipt. Two entry paths, driven by which collection the front-end order came from. Every offer is a one-time price that never repeats: 50% off the compare-at price shown on tanaorjewelry.com. Design and page behavior must match the Plateful example exactly.

Path A · bought from the regular collection

A-U2 and A-D1 sell the same thing at the same price. So do A-U3 and A-D2. The offer is identical; the page copy changes: an upsell page continues the momentum, a downsell page reframes after a "no". A customer never sees the same offer twice.

Path B · bought from the premium collection

Page specs · 6 offer pages + receipt

Nine flow positions, six distinct offer pages plus the receipt. Each downsell is the same layout as its upsell twin with the copy swapped, so the designer builds one template per offer and two copy states.

Both variants are built for every offer page. Checkout Champ splits traffic 50/50 at the first post-purchase page and keeps the customer in the same variant through the whole path. Success metric: post-purchase revenue per front-end order.

Decisions already made

  1. Upgrade backend: swap or add-and-charge-difference, whichever is simpler in Checkout Champ. Frontend must read as an upgrade of the piece just bought.
  2. Discount rule: every offer is 50% off the compare-at price on tanaorjewelry.com. Not "extra 30%". Compare-at prices are pulled from Shopify.
  3. Video on every upsell and downsell page, hidden offer, auto-scroll reveal, exactly like Plateful. Plus a no-video variant of every page for the test.
  4. Bible Box is offered at 50% off as the last step on both paths, only when the cart has no Bible Box.
  5. "More of the same" is the exact variant bought. No color choice, quantity 1.
  6. Mixed cart (regular + premium): Path A. The upgrade targets the highest-value non-premium item in the cart.
  7. Each upsell and each downsell gets its own video. The offer is hidden until the "click now" cue. The customer has to watch to reach the offer.
  8. Premium (14K) offers are made to order, no stock dependency. Serenity is offered to regular-collection buyers only.

Build rules for the developer

  1. Routing reads the front-end order's product collection tag. Regular → Path A. Premium only → Path B.
  2. Pages 1, 2 and 4 are dynamic: product, image, color and price come from the purchased line item and its premium twin. Map each regular SKU to its premium SKU in one lookup table.
  3. Every accept is a one-click charge to the card on file. No re-entering payment. Shipping is merged into the original order, one tracking number.
  4. An offer is shown at most once per order. Back button, refresh or closing the tab must not re-show a declined or accepted offer.
  5. Downsell pages reuse the upsell page template with a second copy state and their own video.
  6. Video reveal: the offer section does not render on load. A timestamp cue on the video, set per page, injects the offer and smooth-scrolls to it. There is no way to reach the offer before the cue. Variant B (straight OTO) has no video and shows the offer on load.
  7. Mixed cart: pick the highest-value line item whose SKU letter 5 is not G, and build the upgrade page from that item and its premium twin.
  8. Premium twin lookup: same SKU with letter 5 replaced by G. Some twins carry different length digits (e.g. P262BW0610000KF → P262GW0550500KF), so use the mapping table below, not string replacement.
  9. No inventory gate on premium offers (A-U1, B-U1): 14K pieces are made to order. Regular offers (A-U2/A-D1, Serenity, Bible Box) check Shopify available quantity and skip the page when it is 0 or below.
  10. Route on the SKU, never on the product. The same premium SKU exists both as a variant on the main product and as a standalone "14k Solid Gold …" product.
  11. Bible Box condition checks for a paid Bible Box line item in the front-end order. Free or $0 gift lines do not count.
  12. Regular SKUs with no premium twin (greyed rows in the table) skip A-U1 and start at A-U2.
  13. Price rule in code: offer = floor(compareAtPrice × 0.5). Bible Box is the one exception at $9.99.
  14. Bible Box step checks the cart for a Bible Box line item and skips itself if found.
  15. The timer is real. When it hits zero, the CTA disables and the page advances to the next step.
  16. Receipt lists every accepted item. Klaviyo order confirmation fires once, after the last offer, not after the front-end charge.
  17. Mobile first. Most Tanaor traffic is Meta on phone. Desktop is the secondary layout.
